# Redesign architecture & inspiration references

Working reference for the `redesign` branch. Companion to [repositioning-feedback.md](./repositioning-feedback.md). Not published — lives outside `src/` and `public/`.

## Reference sites (visited 2026-09-26)

### [Alexa Kaminsky](https://alexakaminsky.com/) — strongest structural reference
- Hero: name, one-line positioning ("A force multiplier for design"), one sentence of proof, then a compact list of role · company · current-flag.
- Work is grouped under capability headers, not chronology or company: **Leadership & Org Design**, **Strategy & Vision**, **Product Design**.
- Every project preview: title, company · role · year(s), 2-3 sentence outcome-oriented summary. No thumbnail imagery needed — text carries it.
- Side Projects and Resources sections after Work — personal/tinkering work kept clearly secondary.
- About section is short (~3 short paragraphs), personal color at the very end, not up front.
- Visual style itself is plain/minimal (serif headline font, cream background, circular headshot) — worth noting but the feedback says borrow structure, not visuals.

### [Brian Verhoeven](https://www.verhoeven.design/work) — content depth reference for design-systems story
- Work index lists case studies with a "showcases:" tag list (Design Leadership, Systems Thinking, Program Management, etc.) before the click-through — sets expectations before you commit to reading.
- The Morningstar Design System case study (the one to emulate for Built/Timber) is structured as:
  - Short intro paragraph (what MDS is + his role in one breath)
  - **What was my role on the project?** (bulleted responsibilities)
  - **What were we working to solve?** (the organizational/political challenge, audience)
  - **What were the results?** (bulleted, quantified: $ secured, adoption across 60+ repos, etc.)
  - Then deep narrative sections with their own subheads: "Using strategy to build buy-in," "Driving organizational change" (includes a memorable anecdote — the "480 button variations" story — and a quoted exec reaction), "Ensuring clear communication happened" (stakeholder cadence diagram), "A focus on team collaboration" (team rituals), "Creating an efficient model for adoption" (adoption framework diagram)
  - Closes with **Lessons Learned**
- Key lesson: the story is framed as organizational change driven through people and process, with the design system as the vehicle — not a component-library showcase. Diagrams/slides are used as evidence, not decoration.

### [Alex Chiu](https://mchiu.co.uk/) — specificity in framing systems/AI work
- Site itself is a playful animated bento-grid (not the layout to copy), but the principle holds: project/skill framing should name the concrete capability or artifact, not a buzzword — e.g. "a design system that AI can use" rather than "AI-enabled design system." Apply this to phrasing/titles, not layout.

### [Brad Weaver](https://www.sbradweaver.com/) — outcome-first card pattern
- Selected Work cards follow a tight formula: **category tag** (e.g. "DESIGN SYSTEMS") → **company** → **title** (plain, specific, e.g. "Enterprise Design System") → 2-3 sentence summary naming the problem, the scope/scale, and a quantified result → "View Case Study."
- Worth borrowing: the compact card formula itself. Not worth borrowing: the busy, consultant/agency tone, logo-wall, testimonials, courses/products sections — our site should stay leaner than this.

## Proposed new homepage architecture (from ChatGPT critique, adapted)

1. **Focused hero**
   - Name + one-line thesis: "I build the teams, systems and product foundations that help complex organizations scale."
   - One supporting sentence (15+ years, enterprise platforms, design systems, design engineering; currently leading distributed design team at Tive).
   - CTAs: View leadership work / Resume / LinkedIn.
   - Compact credibility strip below: 15+ years · 3 senior designers led · 150+ system components · 40%→90% adoption · design + engineering background.

2. **Three featured stories** (large editorial cards, not 5 equal thumbnails)
   - **Leading and scaling product design at Tive** — Team leadership · Operating model · Enterprise platform. Proves: can inherit and lead a platform design team.
   - **Scaling Timber across seven product teams** — Design systems · Governance · Design engineering. Proves: creates organizational leverage, not just components.
   - **Designing operational software around physical reality** (Stitch Fix returns) — Complex workflows · Hands-on design · Measurable impact. Proves: stays close to users/systems/craft.
   - Each card: title, 2-3 word capability tags, one outcome-oriented sentence, italicized "What it proves" line.

3. **"How I lead" section** — four principles, one sentence + one proof point each:
   - Create clarity from ambiguity
   - Develop autonomous designers
   - Build reusable foundations
   - Connect design decisions to delivery

4. **Selected impact** — compact 2-column grid, ~6 stats total:
   - Team/org impact: led distributed senior design team; established critique/validation practices; built cross-functional governance; increased designer ownership.
   - Product/systems impact: design-system adoption ~40%→90%+; accessibility coverage 50%→95%; warehouse throughput +50%; product retention +25%.

5. **Career path, condensed** — replace long "My Design Journey" essay with a visual progression line: Product designer → design engineer → platform designer → systems leader → product design manager, plus 2 sentences on why that progression matters (coaching, deep eng engagement, platform leadership).

6. **Short human ending** — one photo, ~75 words, warmth without delaying the professional story.

## Case-study template (for Tive, Timber, and other deep-dive pages)

Opening summary table, same 6 fields every time:
| Field | Example |
|---|---|
| Challenge | Design quality and decision-making varied across teams |
| My role | Product Design Lead and manager of three senior designers |
| Scope | Web platform, two mobile apps and connected products |
| Team | Design, Product and Engineering partners |
| Leadership move | Established a shared operating model and clearer ownership |
| Outcome | Earlier validation, stronger autonomy and reusable product patterns |

Then narrative structure (borrowing Verhoeven's depth + Weaver's tightness):
1. What I inherited
2. What I recognized
3. The leadership decisions I made
4. How the team participated
5. How the product or organization changed
6. Evidence and outcomes
7. What I would improve next

## Core shift
Current site organizes around **things designed**. Redesign organizes around **changes led**, with product craft shown as evidence inside those stories rather than as the headline.
